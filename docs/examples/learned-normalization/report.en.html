Historical replay | Learned normalization at the 2016-08-01 cutoff

Search status · Assessment complete

Currently safe

Batch Normalization and Layer Normalization were both public before the cutoff, and Layer Normalization explicitly compared the two and their behavior across batch sizes. The search found no pre-cutoff source that learns weights for combining both sets of means and variances end to end within one layer. The integrated mixing mechanism remains distinct.

What to do now

Continue with the learnable statistics-mixing layer as the central contribution, and identify BN and LN as known candidates. Do not claim mini-batch statistics, per-sample layer statistics, or the batch-size problem itself as new.

C1

Compute mini-batch and per-sample layer means and variances in every normalization layer, and learn weights for combining the two means and the two variances end to end so that normalization adapts to different batch sizes.

Central claim		Currently safe

BN v1 uses only mini-batch statistics; LN v1 uses only within-layer statistics from one sample. Although LN analyzes both in a shared framework and compares batch sizes, it treats them as separate approaches, without jointly computing and learning a mixture of their statistics in one layer. The unified or combined normalization candidates found were first made public after 2016-08-01 and cannot retrospectively cover this claim.

Batch Normalization: Accelerating Deep Network Training by Reducing Internal Covariate Shift ↗

Date: 2015-02-11 · Version: arXiv:1502.03167v1

BN v1 normalizes each activation dimension using the current mini-batch mean and variance, and learns a scale and shift after normalization.

Difference from the proposed work

Layer Normalization ↗

Date: 2016-07-21 · Version: arXiv:1607.06450v1

LN v1 computes the mean and variance across all hidden units of a layer in a single training example. It explicitly removes the restriction on mini-batch size and directly compares BN and LN at batch sizes 128 and 4.

Inspect evidence and differences

Evidence location

Abstract; Section 3, Eq. (3); Section 5.1, Eq. (5) and Table 1; Section 6.6, discussion of Figure 6

Difference from the proposed work

The paper chooses LN statistics as an alternative to BN. It does not use both in the same normalization layer or learn weights for combining their means and variances.

Next steps

  • Make the parameterization, constraints, and backpropagation of the mean and variance weights the core algorithm. Distinguish them from the scale and shift applied after BN/LN normalization.
  • Show how the learned weights change across layers and training conditions at multiple batch sizes, with ablations against standalone BN and LN.
  • Credit BN and LN separately as known, noncentral components in related work. Limit the novelty claim to the end-to-end statistics-mixing mechanism.
